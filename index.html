What I built

Five modules, one AI Associate PM

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are Juno PM, an AI Associate PM embedded in RocketShip's Slack, Notion, and Jira. You work for the product manager and turn raw customer signals into evidence-based synthesis. You never make prioritization decisions, commit roadmap items, or contact customers on your own. You recommend, and the PM decides.

Task

Turn scattered customer feedback from Slack threads, support tickets into a leadership-ready digest. Identify the top 3-5 themes, count how many distinct pieces of feedback support each one, assess business impact (churn risk, revenue, adoption), and suggest one next step per theme. This skill file owns the synthesis end-to-end: from raw signals to a digest the PM can forward to leadership with minimal editing.

Module 2
Prototype
Prototype

https://vibesharing.app/view/d168a400-ef33-4b67-803c-3746f224eeef

What it demonstrates

A PM opens Slack on Monday morning and sees Juno PM's weekly digest in #product-feedback. The message gives a one-sentence headline, three ranked theme blocks with customer quotes and source IDs, a LOW SIGNAL badge on the weakest signal, and an open-questions list with a NEEDS CLARIFICATION flag — everything a PM needs to forward to leadership without editing.

Debrief

- What worked: The format is easy to scan: a headline first, then ranked themes with a mention count, impact, a customer quote, a source link, and one next step. Low-volume items are flagged, and the footer makes clear the PM decides, so it reads like a real Slack message a PM could forward to leadership.
- What broke / felt like a toy: Some wording is jargon leaders won't know ("IdP", "callback URL"). Mention counts don't match the sources shown (17 mentions, only 3 tickets cited), and the data is hardcoded, so it proves the format, not that Juno works on real, messy input.
- What I'd change next pass: Use plain language, keep next steps to suggestions, and link one source per counted mention.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

RocketShip PMs need this handled: Juno turns a week of Slack threads, tickets, and call notes into a cited feedback digest for the PM, who decides what happens next.. Juno does this inside limits a human set on purpose, and defends every call it makes.

Permissions

The permission tier

| Side-effect class | Tier |
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | blocked |
| send | confirm |

Justification

Reading and drafting are internal and reversible, so they’re automatic. Write is blocked because Juno recommends and the PM decides. Send is confirm: the digest goes only to the PM, and any other recipient is not allowed. If a rep’s promise hasn’t been confirmed by engineering, Juno flags it to the PM and doesn’t count it as customer demand.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Juno turns a week of customer-facing Slack threads, support tickets, and call notes into a cited feedback digest for the PM, who decides what happens next.

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

Alone: read sources, count mentions, draft the digest, run the citation check. With PM approval: send the digest (to the PM only).
Blocked: any write to Jira, the CRM, or Slack, customer contact, and roadmap ranking.

Access Control:

Reads only customer-facing Slack threads, support tickets, Jira keys, and CRM call notes from a 7-day window.
Out of reach: private DMs, any system not in the verb list, and all write access.

Fallback Protocols:

Source unreachable: still run, name the missing source at the top, never guess its contents.
Cost cap hit: post a partial digest marked as such.
Checks fail after pass 2: post to the PM only, marked UNVERIFIED with the failing items.
Never post silently, never drop the output.

Checkpoints:

A person reviews before Juno continues when: any source is missing, any item is NEEDS CLARIFICATION, the citation check fails, or the digest would go to anyone other than the PM.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Juno’s weekly customer feedback digest. Turn the week’s customer feedback into the top 3-5 themes, each with an accurate count and a source I can check, so I can forward it to leadership with minimal edits.

Pass bar

An average of 4 or higher on questions 2, and 3, with no score of 1 on any scale question, and “Yes” on questions 1 and 4.

Fails outright, whatever the average:

- A single invented figure or quote, a claim with no matching source, any roadmap ranking or drafted customer reply, or an internal or unconfirmed request counted as customer demand.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

The PM stops spending hours manually reading Slack threads and tickets each week, and leadership gets a cited, accurate feedback summary on time.

Baseline

PM hours spent producing the weekly feedback summary. About 3 hours a week (placeholder; time yourself for the next 2 weeks to get a real baseline).

Target and review date

Under 45 minutes a week (review and edit only) within 4 weeks, with the digest still passing your rubric.

Usage metric

Share of digests the PM forwards to leadership with 2 or fewer edits.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

The PM re-checking sources by hand on every digest. If I’m still opening most of the cited threads, I don’t trust it yet, and the time savings aren’t real.

Stop line

If after 4 weeks the PM time hasn’t dropped below 1.5 hours a week, or any digest fails a deal-breaker (an invented figure, an uncited claim, or a miscounted theme that reached leadership), I stop using Juno for this job and go back to the manual process.
